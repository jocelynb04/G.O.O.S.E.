# AMP-H570 amphibian constraint analysis

Run `analysis = run_eco_constraint_analysis;` from the AMP-H570 folder. The returned `reference_power` table reports required shaft power, available shaft power and margin for each powered condition. The mission inputs are in [AMP_H570_requirements.json](../config/AMP_H570_requirements.json). The inherited chart settings are stored locally in [CONVENTIONAL_CONSTRAINT_INPUTS.json](reference/CONVENTIONAL_CONSTRAINT_INPUTS.json), so the hybrid chart can run on its own.

## Reference point and shaded region

- The star uses the default mission's loaded takeoff weight, the retained 279-ft² wing, and the sea-level peak shaft rating. The default values are about 8,999 lb, 32.25 lbf/ft² and 11.78 lbf/shp. All vertical coordinates use peak sea-level shaft power as the denominator, including constraints evaluated with continuous power.
- The upper limit for each power curve is the ratio of condition-available power to peak power divided by required shaft hp per pound of takeoff weight. The green area is below the active powered curves and left of the water wall. It assumes the same power ratios as installed power scales and a fixed amphibian drag polar. The horizontal line is the actual installed rating at the loaded mission weight. Its green segment shows which wing loadings pass the primary screen with that unit. Any resizing would require recalculating weight, aerodynamics and battery integration.
- The first cruise segment supplies the primary mission speed and altitude, 150 KTAS at 10,000 ft by default. The inherited 164-KTAS conventional comparator has its own line and margin. Both use the existing cruise screen at 97.5% of loaded takeoff weight, with no boost or generator shaft load. The 164-KTAS comparator does not set the shaded envelope.

## Climb and go-around

- Both clean climbs require a 3° flight-path angle at takeoff weight. The calculation minimizes shaft power over speed using the inherited parabolic drag polar, with lift equal to weight times the cosine of the angle and the course stall margin enforced. The implied rate in ft/min varies with airspeed. The separate mission schedule still uses its configured climb rate.
- Continuous power is bounded by the combined continuous rating, altitude-adjusted combustion power and propeller limit. See [published ratings and installation assumptions](AMP_H570_ASSUMPTIONS_AND_SOURCES.md). The default model uses 543 shp continuously and up to 764 shp briefly.
- Balked landing uses a 3% gradient, the full landing-flap, gear-up polar, and the mission's ending weight and SOC. It assumes a fresh burst up to the modeled 120-second limit. Usable battery energy above minimum SOC and the pack discharge limit both cap bus power. Hotel load, conversion efficiency, motor limit, combustion power and the combined/propeller cap then bound shaft power. A separate fuel-only curve shows the continuous fallback. The burst is a quasi-steady screen. It does not model transition, cooldown, wind, or clearing an obstacle after touchdown.
- The loss-of-propulsion line shows zero powered climb for loss of the shared propulsive path. For loss of combustion alone, the result includes an ideal electric upper bound and its margin against the reference balked-landing requirement. This assumes the motor can still drive the propeller through an intact drivetrain, with the same battery budget. It does not establish fault isolation, restart capability, or a certified OEI climb.

## Water landing over 50 ft

The conventional proxy uses a 3° approach from 50 ft to a 10-ft flare entry, plus 150 ft for the flare. The remaining distance within the 2,004-ft target is assigned to water deceleration. Effective constant deceleration is inferred at the conventional reference wing loading from the same distance and a touchdown speed of 1.15 times stall speed. The hybrid calculation retains the floats and course full-flap CLmax of 2.2, and substitutes mission-ending weight divided by takeoff weight for the old fixed 0.975 landing fraction. This proxy has no measured water deceleration, float hydrodynamics, or hybrid water takeoff distance. The 2,004-ft calibration datum has no verified over-50-ft water definition.
